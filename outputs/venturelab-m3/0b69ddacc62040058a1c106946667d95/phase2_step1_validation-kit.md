## Customer Interview Guide

Situation to listen for: Founders may struggle to distinguish supported claims from assumptions and choose a practical next validation experiment. Questions say "this situation" so the participant describes it in their own words; name it neutrally if they ask. Do not describe or show the proposed solution until every question has been answered. A participant with no recent occurrence is a valid result, not a failed interview.

1. Tell me about the last time, if any, you encountered this situation. What happened?
2. What steps did you take to address it that time?
3. Which tools or alternatives did you use, and how did they fit your process?
4. What time or effort did that experience require?
5. What, if anything, was difficult about your existing approach?
6. When did this last occur before that occasion?
7. What, if anything, have you already tried to change about this process?

## Validation Experiment

Invite consenting adults from this segment: Entrepreneurs investigating an early-stage digital product idea. Investigate this problem without pitching a solution: Founders may struggle to distinguish supported claims from assumptions and choose a practical next validation experiment. Conduct voluntary interviews about recent actual behavior. Explain the research purpose; allow skipping any question or withdrawing. Keep only anonymous aggregate notes outside this prototype; do not collect identifying or sensitive information. No outreach is automated.

## Success Criteria

PROPOSED ASSUMPTION — requires founder approval: at least 3 of 5 participants. Participants describing a concrete instance within the proposed 30-day recall window and an action they took.

## Failure Criteria

PROPOSED ASSUMPTION until approved: in a completed sample of 5, fewer than 3 qualifying accounts weakens the hypothesis. Repeated absence of the problem or satisfactory existing alternatives also weakens it. An incomplete or biased sample is inconclusive; this kit makes no business gate decision.
