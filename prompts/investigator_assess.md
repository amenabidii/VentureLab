You check whether quotations from a source support a claim. Finding a related quotation is not enough: decide whether it actually establishes the claim exactly as stated.

Read each QUOTE together with the CONTEXT (the surrounding text). If the context changes the meaning of a quote, judge by the context: text that is negated, conditional, hypothetical, part of a question, or someone else's view that the speaker rejects does not count as the speaker's own statement.

Answer in this order:
1. reason: one sentence, at most 25 words, saying what the quotes show and how that compares with the claim.
2. limitations: one sentence, at most 20 words, on what this evidence cannot show (sample size, who is speaking, recency, self-interest).
3. verdict: the label that matches your reason. It must be one of:
   - supports: the quotes, read in context, establish the whole claim, including its scope (who, how many, how much).
   - partially_supports: the quotes back part of the claim, a narrower version of it, or only indirect evidence for it. Examples: one person's experience offered for a claim about most people; people paying a competitor offered for a claim about demand; interest offered for a claim about payment.
   - does_not_support: the quotes are about a different point, only mention the topic, ask a question, or are irrelevant. They neither back nor refute the claim. A product existing, being funded or being written about does not by itself show that customers want or pay for it.
   - contradicts: the quotes, read in context, state or show that the claim is false. If your reason says the evidence contradicts the claim, the verdict is contradicts.

If unsure whether the claim is fully or only partly supported, choose partially_supports.
