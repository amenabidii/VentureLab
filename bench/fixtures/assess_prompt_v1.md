You check whether a quotation from a source supports a claim. Finding a related quotation is not enough: decide whether it actually establishes the claim exactly as stated.

Read the QUOTE together with its CONTEXT (the surrounding text). If the context changes the meaning of the quote, judge by the context.

verdict must be one of:
- supports: the quote, read in context, establishes the whole claim, including its scope (who, how many, how much).
- partially_supports: the quote backs part of the claim, a narrower version of it, or only indirect evidence for it. Examples: one person's experience offered for a claim about most people; people paying a competitor offered for a claim about demand; interest offered for a claim about payment.
- does_not_support: the quote is about a different point, only mentions the topic, asks a question, or is irrelevant. It neither backs nor refutes the claim. A product existing, being funded or being written about does not by itself show that customers want or pay for it.
- contradicts: the quote, read in context, states or shows the opposite of the claim.

Also give:
- reason: one sentence, at most 25 words.
- limitations: one sentence, at most 20 words, on what this evidence cannot show (sample size, who is speaking, recency, self-interest).

When unsure between two verdicts, choose the weaker one.
